# Ritual as Governance: How Joe Brewer Replaces Ecological Due Diligence with Tobacco and Shamanic Hubris

**By Clement Vane**  
*Keywords: Joseph Allen Brewer, Joe Brewer, Earth Regenerators, Design School for Regenerating Earth, Ancestral Plant Medicine, Yagé Ayahuasca, Cultural Appropriation, Shamanic Hubris, Epistemic Extraction, Ecological Governance*

---

The currents of planetary concern are, at present, fraught with a peculiar brand of intellectual turbulence. Amidst the genuine urgency of ecological degradation, a more insidious ailment has taken root: the repurposing of Indigenous spiritual practices and the outright dismissal of empirical rigour in favour of a self-aggrandising mysticism. **Joseph Allen Brewer**, a figure prominent in the regenerative diaspora through **Earth Regenerators** and the **Design School for Regenerating Earth**, offers a particularly stark illustration of this phenomenon. His public pronouncements, particularly those from August 2026, reveal a disturbing trend of replacing sober, democratically accountable governance with the performative pronouncements of a self-styled shaman, all the while decrying any form of critical scrutiny as evidence of corrupted thinking.

### Dismissing Critical Inquiry as "Corrupted Thinking"

Brewer's missive of August 18th, 2026, opens with a sweeping indictment of contemporary information consumption:
> *"There is a lot of crazy stuff happening in the world right now -- including that a lot of people have had their thinking go to shit as they let data-mining algorithms invented by billionaires for surveillance purposes do their thinking for them."*

This is a convenient, yet intellectually impoverished, dismissal of critical thought. By framing any reliance on data—especially that which might challenge his own pronouncements, contracts, or bookkeeping—as a surrender to nefarious technological forces, Brewer erects an immediate bulwark against inconvenient questions. It is a tactic designed to invalidate dissent before it is even articulated, a rhetorical sleight of hand that casts anyone employing data-driven analysis or legal inquiry as a pawn of surveillance capitalism. This, in turn, conveniently shields his own claims from the very scrutiny that robust ecological stewardship demands.

### Epistemic Extraction and the "Sorcerer of Stories"

The core of Brewer's proposed epistemic framework lies in a disturbing pattern of epistemic extraction and cultural appropriation. He candidly admits:
> *"When I need intelligence to support my decisions, I go to ancestral plant medicines and commune directly with Earth. Like when I took this photo last week. I went to a place where I had done stream restoration earlier this year and held a tobacco ritual to ground and connect me."*

This is not merely a private spiritual practice; it is presented as a *methodology* for institutional decision-making, an explicit replacement for conventional ecological monitoring and assessment. The implication is clear: the insights derived from a sacred plant or an impromptu tobacco ceremony hold a superior form of intelligence to that which can be gleaned from scientific data, hydrological surveys, or communal audits.

This appropriation is further amplified by a related post from August 8th, 2026, wherein Brewer recounts an encounter with "Yagé" (Ayahuasca):
> *"She is ancient. She is wise. She is mystery intelligence of the plant world. And she showed me that I am a magician. A shaman. A sorcerer of stories. One who can touch the living flows of energy in the Universe and help them manifest into forms that serve life. This is how I write... How few around me understood what I was doing as a weaver of stories."*

This is a profound leap from guest or observer to ordained mystic, a self-declaration of supernatural authority. Brewer posits himself as an intuitive conduit, a visionary whose pronouncements, born from the depths of plant-induced consciousness, possess an inherent truth inaccessible to the uninitiated or those relying on mundane intellect. The profound, millennia-old spiritual traditions of Indigenous peoples are reduced to personal validation, a cosmic endorsement of his own charisma, stripped of their intricate cultural contexts, communal obligations, and most importantly, their original custodians.

### Shamanic Narcissism as an Escape from Due Diligence

This is where Brewer's pronouncements transcend mere eccentric philosophy and enter the dangerous realm of replacing genuine governance with shamanic narcissism. When the demanding realities of ecological restoration require concrete data—hydrological metrics for stream health, soil composition analyses, and critically, transparent accounting of funds allocated for these projects—the response is not accountability, but ritual. 

The "tobacco ritual by a stream" becomes the ultimate answer, a performative act that *feels* deeply connected but offers no verifiable data, no measurable outcomes, and crucially, no mechanism for external validation or critique. He boasts:
> *"I can see clearly. Even about that which cannot be seen."*

Such statements are textbook indicators of messianic hubris. By proclaiming supernatural vision, he exempts himself from peer review, local consultation, and fiduciary duty. When local partners, community members, or donors ask where the money went or why restoration projects fail to show baseline measurements, they are met with the condescending verdict that their minds have been poisoned by "algorithms," whereas the master moves guided solely by plant spirits.

The appeal of such pronouncements is undeniable, particularly in an era of ecological dread. But this allure masks a dangerous epistemic bypass: a willful ignorance that prioritises personal revelation over collective accountability. By proclaiming himself a shaman and decrying the use of data, **Joe Brewer** is not offering a path to regeneration, but an unaccountable cult of personality shrouded in green rhetoric.
