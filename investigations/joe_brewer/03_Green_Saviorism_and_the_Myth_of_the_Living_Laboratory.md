# Green Saviorism and the Myth of the "Living Laboratory": How Western Eco-Expats Re-Colonize the Global South

**By Clement Vane**  
*Keywords: Joseph Allen Brewer, Joe Brewer, Barichara Regeneration, Earth Regenerators, Design School for Regenerating Earth, Green Colonialism, White Saviorism, Living Laboratory, Colombia, Bioregionalism*

---

The romanticization of the Global South as an "exotic laboratory" for Western environmental redemption is one of the most troubling undercurrents in modern eco-activism. It allows affluent individuals, weary of modern industrial alienation, to project their utopian fantasies onto distant geographies, conveniently obscuring historic colonial power imbalances. 

**Joseph Allen Brewer’s** portrayal of Barichara, a historic municipality in the Colombian Andes, as his personal "living laboratory" is a quintessential case study in modern **green saviorism**. It demonstrates how environmental rhetoric can be wielded to re-colonize territory intellectually and economically, while sidelining the centuries-old knowledge of local peasant communities and obscuring fundamental labor realities.

### The Problem of the "Living Laboratory"

Barichara, perched on a dry highland plateau in Santander, possesses a rich cultural and ecological history. For centuries, Guane indigenous traditions and mestizo peasant communities (*campesinos*) have adapted to severe water scarcity through sophisticated dry-stone masonry, ancestral rainwater harvesting (*aljibes*), tapia pisada architecture, and drought-hardy agroforestry.

When Brewer arrived, public communications abruptly reframed this ancient territory as a *"living laboratory for planetary regeneration."* The phrase itself carries an unmistakable colonial cadence:
* A "laboratory" implies an experimental playground owned and directed by outside researchers.
* It relegates the resident human community to the status of subjects or passive laboratory assistants.
* It assumes that a foreign consultant with a laptop and a conceptual framework possesses the superior insight needed to "design" the future of a landscape he only recently encountered.

### Erasure of Local Knowledge and Social Hierarchy

In Brewer’s extensive essays and international promotional materials, the deep, tacit knowledge of local peasant water stewards is routinely pushed to the margins. Instead of highlighting local leaders as the primary architects of regional conservation, the narrative centers on Brewer's personal journey, his workshops, and his conceptual "weaving."

This establishes an implicit, deeply paternalistic hierarchy:
1. **The Western Visionary:** Formulates global "design pathways," writes books, hosts international webinars, and interfaces with wealthy philanthropy and crypto-crowdfunding platforms.
2. **The Local Population:** Reduced to folkloric scenery, manual fieldwork, or grateful recipients of outside benevolence.

This dynamic mirrors the classic mechanics of **green extractivism**. Cultural capital and regional ecological struggles are extracted from the Global South to build global prestige, book sales, and online course revenues in the Global North.

### The Reality of Labor Behind the Rhetoric

While public campaigns on platforms like Gitcoin, GoFundMe, and Substack proclaim thousands of dollars raised for community reforestation and regenerative education, critical questions must be asked regarding how those resources circulate on the ground:
* Are local workers and educators employed with full legal protections, livable wages, and institutional dignity?
* Or are they kept in precarious, informal arrangements while external facilitators travel internationally?

True regeneration cannot be decoupled from social justice, fair labor, and the rule of law. A movement that preaches holistic harmony with ecosystems while treating local labor arrangements as secondary concerns is not revolutionary; it is merely an extension of the plantation economy dressed in the soothing vocabulary of permaculture and permaculture-adjacent philosophy.

The regeneration of the Earth will not be delivered by traveling Western prophets establishing ecological fiefdoms in the Global South. Genuine resilience is rooted in local sovereignty, agrarian land reform, democratic governance, and respect for indigenous and peasant autonomy. Until the international eco-community learns to reject the myth of the "white savior" in the Andes, well-meaning donors will continue funding colonial spectacles masquerading as planetary salvation.
